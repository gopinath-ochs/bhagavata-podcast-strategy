The Bhagavata Podcast Is Growing —
Here's What That Looks Like

A short, friendly update for the scholars, guests, and supporters who make this show possible.

If you've been a guest on the podcast, or you've simply cheered it on from a distance, thank you. Six months ago we sat down to figure out how to grow this show — and now we have real numbers to show you what happened. Here's the story, in plain terms.

The Short Version

People are finding the podcast, watching more of it, and coming back for more — across YouTube, Apple Podcasts, and Spotify. Growth isn't explosive, but it's real, and it's the kind that tends to keep building.

Where People Are Actually Listening

One of the most interesting things we learned is that the podcast doesn't have one audience — it has four, and they don't look alike at all.

🇮🇳 Spotify — 59 in every 100 listeners are in India
🇺🇸 YouTube — 28 in every 100 viewers are in the US
🇺🇸 Apple Podcasts — mostly US, with India, the UK, and Australia next

On YouTube, most viewers are men in their late 20s and 30s. On Spotify, the audience is older and much more evenly split between men and women. In other words: this show is reaching very different kinds of people depending on where they find it — which is a wonderful sign for a text as universal as the Bhagavata.

The nicest surprise: people who listen, really listen

On Apple Podcasts, listeners are sticking with episodes for almost half to over three-quarters of the whole conversation — some episodes even more. That's unusually high for an hour-long, scholarly discussion. It tells us the people who commit to listening are genuinely engaged, not just passing through.

What's Working

Better titles are getting more people to click

Back in March, we noticed the episode titles read like a syllabus — helpful if you already knew the material, invisible if you didn't. We rewrote them as real questions: "Who Created the Creator?" instead of "2.5 with Sri Prahlada Dasa." It worked. Out of every 100 people who see an episode thumbnail, about 5 or 6 now click on it — compared to about 4 before we changed the titles.

A small-sounding change — 4.3 to 5.5 — is actually a 27% jump. That's the difference a good question in the title makes.

Women guests are landing especially well

Episodes featuring Manjari Devi Dasi and Anuradha Devi Dasi have performed above average on every measure we track. If you know a woman scholar-practitioner who'd be a wonderful guest, that's a warm invitation.

The hour-long format is not the problem

We'd worried that a full hour was too long for casual viewers. The data says otherwise — some episodes hold nearly a third of their viewers for the entire runtime, and Apple listeners routinely stay for most of an episode. The depth of these conversations is a strength, not an obstacle.

What We're Still Working On

The first 30 seconds lose almost half the audience

Out of every 100 people who click play, about 53 are still watching 30 seconds in — and that's true whether the episode goes on to be one of our best or one of our quietest. So it isn't about any one guest or topic. It's simply the moment when a curious click either turns into real listening, or doesn't. We're working on stronger, more inviting openings to change that.

How You Can Help

None of this happens without the guests and supporters who give their time, scholarship, and voice to this project. If you'd like to help the podcast reach further, here's what actually makes a difference:

  • Share your episode with your own students, colleagues, or community — a personal recommendation travels further than any algorithm.
  • Leave a rating or a kind comment on Apple Podcasts or Spotify if you enjoyed a conversation — these genuinely help new listeners discover the show.
  • Tell us if you know a guest we should have on — especially women scholar-practitioners, whose episodes have performed wonderfully so far.
  • If you belong to a temple, sangha, or study group, consider sharing an episode as a discussion starter — we're building simple study guides to make that easier.
